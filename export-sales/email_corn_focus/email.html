Where US corn sales stand for the four main buyers against USDA's 3,275 mln bu, on a 10-year pace. Data: USDA export sales through week 11 (w/e 12 Nov). USDA's number is converted to the weekly-sales basis (80.4 Mt).

This week: net sales 1.59 Mt, of which Mexico 0.93 Mt (a second big week), Korea 0.20, Japan 0.14, Europe 0.10.

In one line: to reach 3,275, every buyer has to book about 55% more than its 10-year normal from here. Mexico and Europe are doing that today; Korea is roughly on pace; Japan and the smaller buyers are well behind.

"Needed" shares USDA's total out in proportion to each buyer's usual 10-year bookings from this point, and follows the normal seasonal pattern. Unknown destinations are not assigned to a buyer; the history already reflects them being named later.

What it shows

  • USDA's 3,275 needs a record or near-record year from all the main buyers at once: Japan at a record, Mexico and Korea at their second highest.
  • Mexico is now clearly ahead after two big weeks (2.0 Mt and 0.9 Mt), and is the buyer most likely to deliver its share.
  • Japan is the clearest shortfall: it needs ~385 kt/week to end-February and is booking ~155, well below even its normal pace.
  • The smaller buyers are well behind (~340 vs ~725 kt/week), and together they are the largest share of what is still needed.

What to watch each Thursday: Mexico holding above ~475 kt/week, Japan recovering towards ~385, and the smaller buyers picking up. Without all three, the case for a USDA cut keeps building.
