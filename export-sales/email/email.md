**Subject: US soybean exports 2026/27: we see ~1,825 mln bu vs USDA 1,720 — drivers, odds and weekly signposts**

Team,

**Bottom line**

- Our base case is **~1,825 mln bu**, **~105 above USDA's 1,720** (Nov WASDE). We put the probability that final exports beat 1,720 at **~82%**.
- **China is the swing factor.** At 25 Mt, USDA looks too low. USDA's number only holds if China stops at **~22 Mt** (it already has 19.3 Mt booked including its share of Unknown), or if the rest of world books less from here than in its weakest season of the last 10.
- **Main downside risk:** Brazil, with a record crop and less China demand, competing harder for non-China buyers. If it repeats last season's record pace, our number drops to **~1,780**, still above USDA.
- **If we're right, US ending stocks are ~195 mln bu, not 300** (stocks/use ~4% vs 6.5%), assuming crush is unchanged. That's the monetisable angle: USDA has room to raise exports again in the coming reports.

---

**What we did (5 steps)**

1. **Data:** USDA weekly export sales by destination, 10 seasons (2016/17–2025/26), plus this season through week 9 (w/e 29 Oct). We also used 10 seasons of monthly customs exports for the US and Brazil, by destination.
2. **Pace by region:** for every region we measured what it has booked so far and what it normally books from this week to season end. A simple "share booked by now" multiplier gave errors of ±12 Mt in backtests; this method held at about ±3 Mt across all 10 seasons.
3. **China fixed at 25 Mt:** China's purchases are driven by policy, so we treat China as an input and model everyone else from history. Unknown destinations are split 60% China / 40% rest of world.
4. **Same basis as USDA:** weekly export sales run ~2.3% below USDA's official (Census) exports, so we adjust using 10 seasons of official finals.
5. **Brazil switching:** using 10 seasons of US vs Brazil shipments by destination, we separated buyers that switch origin from buyers whose US volume moves with their own total demand. We then measured how much US volume is at risk if Brazil sells more to non-China buyers.

**Caveats**

- 25 Mt for China is an assumption, not a forecast; ±1 Mt = ±38 mln bu.
- 10 seasons is still a short history, and Brazil competition is measured by volume (no FOB spread data yet), so treat country-level switching estimates as indicative.
- Customs data by destination differs from export sales data within the EU (Netherlands vs Germany). The EU total is consistent.
- Data are through week 9; we update weekly.
