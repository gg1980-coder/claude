**Subject: US corn and soybean exports: what the sales pace says about USDA revisions into the January WASDE**

Hi all,

A short note on how I am tracking US corn and soybean exports, and what I expect from USDA in the December and January WASDE reports.

**What I am doing**

- Every Thursday I take USDA's weekly export sales (all destinations, 10 seasons of history) and compare this season's sales with what each export number needs from here.
- From that I build a **pace estimate** of full-year exports: sales so far + what buyers normally book from this point of the season.
- I have tested this against **16 years of WASDE reports (2010–2026)**: how USDA revises its export forecast after November, how big the steps are, in which months they come, and whether the pace pointed to the direction in advance.

**Where things stand (week 10, w/e 5 November; mln bu)**

| | USDA November | My pace estimate | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corn | 3,275 | ~2,500 | about −800 |
| Soybeans | 1,720 | ~1,760–1,820 (China capped at 25 Mt) | about +40 to +100 |

**What 16 years of WASDE history show**

| | Corn | Soybeans |
|---|---|---|
| Pace pointed the right way for the later USDA revision | 7 of 10 seasons | 8 of 10 seasons |
| How much USDA usually moves | close to the full gap, but sizes vary a lot | about a third of the gap |
| Typical monthly step | 50 mln bu | 20 mln bu |
| December report | usually unchanged (about 6 seasons in 10) | usually unchanged (about 7 in 10) |
| Share of the total revision done by the January report | about half, in seasons that ended with a cut | little: most soybean changes come Feb–May |

In short: **USDA moves gradually and in the direction of the sales pace, but rarely all at once.** The December report usually holds; January is the first report where changes become likely.

**Expected pattern into the January report**

*Corn (USDA 3,275)*

| | December WASDE | January WASDE (cumulative from November) |
|---|---|---|
| Unchanged | **~75–80%** | ~20% |
| Lower | ~15% (−25 to −75) | **~60%** (most likely −50 to −150; about 1 in 3 of those −200 or more) |
| Higher | ~5–10% | ~20% |
| Most likely level | **3,275** | **~3,150–3,225** |

The closest past case is 2022/23: pace well below USDA, USDA cut −75 in December and −150 in January, then kept cutting through August (−488 in total). I expect the full-season cut this year to be ~300–500, ending ~2,750–2,950, but spread over several reports.

*Soybeans (USDA 1,720)*

| | December WASDE | January WASDE (cumulative from November) |
|---|---|---|
| Unchanged | **~65–70%** | **~40%** |
| Higher | ~20% (+10 to +25) | ~30% (+10 to +50) |
| Lower | ~10–15% | ~30% |
| Most likely level | **1,720** | **~1,720–1,745** |

For soybeans the pace leans up, but USDA usually waits: most soybean revisions come between February and May. By the January report I expect little change. Over the full season I see ~55–70% odds that USDA ends above 1,720, most likely ~1,720–1,780.

**What would change this view (weekly sales to watch)**

| | Points to a larger/earlier move | Points to USDA holding |
|---|---|---|
| Corn | Sales back near the 5-yr average (~1.4 Mt/week) through December → January cut more likely, and larger | Sales of ~2 Mt/week or more (the pace USDA needs) → USDA can hold 3,275 into January |
| Soybeans | Rest of world staying above ~610 kt/week and China moving steadily towards 25 Mt → earlier increase | China stalling well short of 25 Mt, or rest-of-world sales fading → risk turns to the downside |

**Caveats**

- The test covers 10 seasons for pace and 16 for USDA's revision pattern: good enough for direction and timing, not for precise sizes.
- For corn, the last two seasons had late-season surges that both USDA and the pace underestimated. That is the main risk to the corn call.
- For soybeans, the call depends on China landing near its 25 Mt purchase commitment.
