**Subject: US soybean exports 2026/27: why we think 1,700 mln bu is too low (for discussion at next week's meeting)**

Hi [name],

Ahead of next week's meeting, here is why we think your **1,700 mln bu** US export assumption for 2026/27 is too low. Our working number is **~1,800**, and USDA's November WASDE has **1,720** (raised by 25, citing the early-season pace). Everything below comes from USDA's weekly export sales data for the last 10 seasons, at the same point of the season (week 9, end of October). We'd like to use it as the basis for the discussion.

**1. What 1,700 implies, in numbers**

1,700 mln bu = 46.3 Mt on USDA's basis ≈ **45.2 Mt** on the weekly export-sales basis (which usually runs ~2.3% lower).

| At week 9 (w/e 29 Oct) | This season | What 1,700 implies | Last 10 seasons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sold so far (shipped + unshipped) | **30.2 Mt** | **67% of the year already sold** | 42%–80%, avg 56%; 9 of 10 seasons below 67% |
| Shipped so far | **12.0 Mt** | **27% of the year already shipped** | 18%–27%, avg 22% |
| Still to sell from here | — | **15.0 Mt** | 12.1–25.2 Mt, avg 22.0; 9 of 10 seasons ~20 Mt or more |

The only season that sold less than 15 Mt after week 9 was **2020/21**. That year China front-loaded record purchases and the US ran short of beans in the spring. Nothing points to a supply squeeze like that this year.

[Chart 1: what 1,700 requires vs 10 seasons of history]

**2. With China at 25 Mt, 1,700 needs the weakest rest-of-world season in 10 years, and then some**

- China has **19.3 Mt** booked (including 60% of the "Unknown" destinations, which historically end up in China).
- If China ends at 25 Mt, **the rest of the world needs to book only 11.5 Mt from here** to reach 1,700.
- In the last 10 seasons the rest of the world booked **13.1–20.5 Mt** after week 9 (avg 16.5 Mt; last 5 years 15.8 Mt). **1,700 sits below every one of the 10 seasons, 12% under the weakest (2023/24).**
- The current pace points the other way. Rest-of-world sales over the last 4 weeks averaged **689 kt/week**, against ~520 kt/week needed for 1,700 and a 5-year average of ~715 kt/week.

**3. Or, if the rest of the world books normally, 1,700 needs China to stop now**

- With the rest of the world at its normal (5-year) pace, 1,700 implies China ends the season at **~20.8 Mt**.
- China already has 19.3 Mt. So **1,700 assumes China buys only ~1.5 Mt more** for the rest of the season, against a 25 Mt purchase commitment.

**4. Every pace method we tested lands above 1,700**

| Method (all backtested on 10 seasons) | Full year (mln bu) | Chance of finishing above 1,700 |
|---|---|---|
| Our working number (China 25 Mt, Brazil competing harder than normal) | **1,800** | **81%** |
| Base case (China 25 Mt, normal Brazil) | 1,825 | 86% |
| Regional pace, 5-yr (China 25 Mt) | 1,859 | 91% |
| Regional pace, 10-yr (China on its own pace) | 1,963 | — |
| Classic multiplier ("share sold by now"), 10-yr | 1,992 | — |
| Each of the last 10 seasons' patterns applied to this year (China 25 Mt) | 1,758–2,038 | **10 of 10 above 1,700** |

Our regional method was within about ±3 Mt (±110 mln bu) of the final number across the 10 seasons. 1,700 is at the bottom edge of that band around our number.

**5. Brazil is the real risk, but not enough on its own to get to 1,700**

- Outside China, the US still holds ~9 Mt of March–August business (31% market share), and that is the volume Brazil competes for.
- We measured which buyers switch to Brazil (Netherlands, Bangladesh, Taiwan, S. Korea, Thailand, Turkey) and how much US business they still have to book.
- Even if Brazil ships **33.5 Mt to non-China buyers** (a new record, +7 Mt vs its 5-year average, and the forecast in the monthly trade dataset we use), our number falls to **~1,735, still above 1,700**.
- The same dataset carries a 2026/27 US export forecast of **49.5 Mt (≈1,819 mln bu) with China at 25.0 Mt**. If that is your dataset, it would be worth reconciling with the 1,700.

**6. Why it matters**

The difference between 1,700 and 1,800 is ~100 mln bu of US ending stocks: **~320 mln bu at 1,700 vs ~220 at 1,800** (USDA: 300 at 1,720, with crush unchanged). That is the difference between a comfortable and a tight US balance sheet.

**What would have to be true for 1,700 (any one of these)**

1. China stops at ~21 Mt despite a 25 Mt commitment, or large cancellations of the current China/Unknown book.
2. The rest of the world books less from here than in any of the last 10 seasons (≤11.5 Mt vs a 13.1 Mt minimum).
3. Brazil takes far more than a record share of non-China demand: well beyond 33.5 Mt to non-China buyers, combined with weak total demand.
