**Subject: US soybean exports 2026/27: why we think 1,700 mln bu is too low (for discussion at next week's meeting)**

Hi [name],

Ahead of next week's meeting, here is why we think your **1,700 mln bu** US export assumption for 2026/27 is too low. Our base case is **1,760**, and it is deliberately conservative: with China at 25 Mt, it assumes the rest of the world books about 10% less than its 5-year average from here. USDA's November WASDE has **1,720** (raised by 25, citing the early-season pace). Everything below comes from USDA's weekly export sales data for the last 10 seasons, at the same point of the season (week 9, end of October). We'd like to use it as the basis for the discussion.

**1. What 1,700 implies, in numbers**

1,700 mln bu = **46.3 Mt**.

| At week 9 (w/e 29 Oct) | This season | What 1,700 implies | Last 10 seasons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sold so far (shipped + unshipped) | **30.2 Mt** | **65% of the year already sold** | 42%–80%, avg 56%; 9 of 10 seasons below 65% |
| Shipped so far | **12.0 Mt** | **26% of the year already shipped** | 18%–27%, avg 22% |
| Still to sell from here | — | **16.0 Mt** | 12.1–25.2 Mt, avg 22.0; 9 of 10 seasons ~20 Mt or more |

The only season that sold less than 16 Mt after week 9 was **2020/21**. That year China front-loaded record purchases and the US ran short of beans in the spring. Nothing points to a supply squeeze like that this year.

[Chart 1: what 1,700 requires vs 10 seasons of history]

**2. With China at 25 Mt, 1,700 needs a rest-of-world season weaker than any in the last 10 years**

- China has **19.3 Mt** booked (including 60% of the "Unknown" destinations, which historically end up in China).
- If China ends at 25 Mt, **the rest of the world needs to book only 12.6 Mt from here** to reach 1,700.
- In the last 10 seasons the rest of the world booked **13.1–20.5 Mt** after week 9 (avg 16.5 Mt; last 5 years 15.8 Mt). **1,700 sits below every one of the 10 seasons**, under even the weakest (2023/24).
- Our 1,760 base case assumes **14.2 Mt**, below the 5-year average and in the bottom half of the 10 seasons.
- The current pace is above both. Rest-of-world sales over the last 4 weeks averaged **689 kt/week**. That compares with ~570 kt/week needed for 1,700, ~645 for our 1,760, and a 5-year average of ~715.

**3. Or, if the rest of the world books normally, 1,700 needs China to stop well short of 25 Mt**

- With the rest of the world at its normal (5-year) pace, 1,700 implies China ends the season at **~21.8 Mt**.
- China already has 19.3 Mt. So **1,700 assumes China buys only ~2.5 Mt more** for the rest of the season, against a 25 Mt purchase commitment.

**4. Every method we tested lands above 1,700**

| Method (all backtested on 10 seasons) | Full year (mln bu) | Chance of finishing above 1,700 |
|---|---|---|
| **Our base case** (China 25 Mt; rest of world ~10% below its 5-yr pace) | **1,760** | **71%** |
| China 25 Mt, rest of world at its 5-yr pace | 1,817 | 85% |
| Same, with Brazil shipping a record 33.5 Mt to non-China buyers | 1,728 | — |
| Regional pace, 10-yr (China on its own pace) | 1,918 | — |
| Classic multiplier ("share sold by now"), 10-yr | 1,947 | — |
| Each of the last 10 seasons' patterns applied to this year (China 25 Mt) | 1,718–1,992 | **10 of 10 above 1,700** |

Our regional method was within about ±3 Mt (±110 mln bu) of the final number across the 10 seasons. So 1,700 is not impossible: it sits inside the error band of our conservative 1,760. But it is below **every** outcome built from actual history, and it needs one of the conditions in section 6 to happen.

**5. Brazil is the real risk, but not enough on its own to get to 1,700**

- Outside China, the US still holds ~9 Mt of March–August business (31% market share), and that is the volume Brazil competes for.
- We measured which buyers switch to Brazil (Netherlands, Bangladesh, Taiwan, S. Korea, Thailand, Turkey) and how much US business they still have to book.
- Even if Brazil ships **33.5 Mt to non-China buyers** (a new record, +7 Mt vs its 5-year average, and the forecast in the monthly trade dataset we use), the model gives **~1,728**, still above 1,700.
- The same dataset carries a 2026/27 US export forecast of **49.5 Mt (≈1,819 mln bu) with China at 25.0 Mt**. If that is your dataset, it would be worth reconciling with the 1,700.

**6. Why it matters, and what would have to be true for 1,700**

The difference between 1,700 and 1,760 is ~60 mln bu of US ending stocks: **~320 mln bu at 1,700 vs ~260 at 1,760** (USDA: 300 at 1,720, with crush unchanged).

For 1,700 to be right, one of these has to happen:

1. China stops at ~22 Mt despite a 25 Mt commitment, or large cancellations of the current China/Unknown book.
2. The rest of the world books less from here than in any of the last 10 seasons (≤12.6 Mt vs a 13.1 Mt minimum).
3. Brazil takes far more than a record share of non-China demand: well beyond 33.5 Mt to non-China buyers, combined with weak total demand.
