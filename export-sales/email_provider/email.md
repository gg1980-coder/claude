**What this week's report added**

**Why we think the risk is to the upside**

- **The current pace is above both numbers.** Over the last 4 weeks, the rest of the world booked ~25% more than 1,700 needs and ~20% more than 1,720 needs.
- **The strength is broad.** Europe, the Middle East & N. Africa, South Asia and Southeast Asia are ahead (table below). Only Mexico and East Asia are weak, and they were again small this week.
- **The alternative explanation is China.** If the rest of the world books normally, 1,700 implies China stops at ~21.8 Mt (only ~1.9 Mt more than today's 19.9 Mt), against a 25 Mt commitment. 1,720 implies ~22.3 Mt.
- **Brazil alone doesn't close the gap.** Even with a record 33.5 Mt of Brazilian exports to non-China buyers, our model gives ~1,730.
- **Probability:** on our model (10-season backtest, error about ±3 Mt), there is a ~73% chance of finishing above 1,700 and ~66% above 1,720. 1,700 is possible, but it is the low end.

**How we calculated this, in simple terms**

1. **What 1,700 leaves for the rest of the world.** 1,700 mln bu is 46.3 Mt. Take off China at 25 Mt and what the rest of the world has already booked (9.4 Mt, shipped and unshipped). What remains, **11.9 Mt**, is what the rest of the world still has to buy between now and the end of August. The same calculation gives 12.4 Mt for 1,720 and 13.5 Mt for 1,760.
2. **What the rest of the world actually bought from this point in past seasons.** For each of the last 10 seasons, we took its final exports to the rest of the world and subtracted what it had booked at the same week (week 10). That gives a range of 12.6–19.9 Mt. There is no model here: these are actual outcomes.
3. **The comparison.** 11.9 Mt and 12.4 Mt are both below all 10 seasons. Chart 1 uses only these two steps. Chart 2 is simpler still: each season's sales at week 10 divided by its final exports.
4. **Regions and weekly pace.** To split the requirement by region, we shared it out in proportion to what each region normally buys from this point (5-year average). The weekly figures and milestones spread it across the coming weeks using the usual seasonal pattern. These figures are indicative; the headline comparison does not depend on them.
5. **Unknown destinations.** We count 60% of "Unknown" as China, in line with history. This does not change the rest-of-world requirement, because China is fixed at 25 Mt in total.
6. **One simplification that favours 1,700.** We converted 1,700 straight to tonnes. Weekly sales data usually end the year about 2% below the official Census export total, so on a like-for-like basis 1,700 would need only ~10.8 Mt from the rest of the world, even further below history.
7. **Probability.** The ~73% / ~66% figures come from how far our method's forecast at this point of the season was from the final number in each of the last 10 seasons (about ±3 Mt).
