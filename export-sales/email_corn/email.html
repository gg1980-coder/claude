Subject: US corn exports 2026/27: the sales pace points to ~2,500–2,700 mln bu, not 3,275. What would need to change

Hi team,

A note on US corn exports ahead of the next update. On USDA's weekly export sales through week 10 (w/e 5 November), the sales pace does not support 3,275 mln bu (USDA November WASDE; our current number: [X]). We think the number should move down towards ~2,500–2,700 mln bu, unless sales accelerate sharply over the next 6–8 weeks. Below is what is happening, and the levels that would keep 3,275 alive.

In one line: sales are at a normal pace, but 3,275 assumes a near-record year. Buyers would need to book more from here than in any of the last 10 seasons, including last year's record.

  • Sales are normal, the forecast is not. Sales are 2% above the 5-yr average but 28% below last year. 3,275 is only 4.5% below last year's record.
  • Shipments are strong. At +51% vs the 5-yr average, they are what USDA's "shipments and sales to date" refers to. But the unshipped book is 19% below the 5-yr average, so the strong shipments are using up the book rather than reflecting new demand.
  • 3,275 needs 52.8 Mt more from here, about 11% more than the record 47.7 Mt booked after week 10 last year, and 60% more than the 10-season average.
  • The recent jump is mostly one Mexico week. Sales were 2.9 Mt in w/e 5 Nov, of which Mexico 2.0 Mt. The three weeks before ran at 1.3–1.5 Mt, close to normal. 3,275 needs ~2.1 Mt every week through November.

A caution: for corn, this method is less precise than for soybeans (±290 mln bu at this point of the season). The last three seasons all finished above it, because late-season buying grew each year (33, 39, 48 Mt after week 10). So we would not lean on the 2%. The robust point is that 3,275 needs a late season bigger than last year's record. ~2,500–2,700 is where the data sit today; the upside risk is a repeat of last year's late run.

How we'd use it

  1. To keep 3,275, we would want to see ~2.1 Mt of sales a week through November, and at least ~41.5 Mt sold by 24 December. That needs Mexico and Japan on a record pace and last year's late-season buyers (Korea, Taiwan, Spain, Unknown) coming back.
  2. If sales track the 2,500–2,750 columns into late December (36.5–38 Mt by 24 Dec), we suggest moving the number to that range.
  3. In between (39–41 Mt by 24 Dec) points to ~3,000, and we'd revisit in January.

Our suggestion: move towards ~2,700 now, with the 24 December checkpoint to confirm. Happy to discuss. We'd also like to understand what is behind your current number, in particular the Mexico and Japan assumptions and how much of last year's late-season demand you expect to return.

How we calculated this, in short

  1. Still to sell = USDA's 3,275 converted to tonnes (adjusted ~3% for the usual gap between weekly sales data and USDA's official export totals), minus what has already been sold this season.
  2. History = for each of the last 10 seasons, final exports minus what had been sold by week 10. These are actual outcomes, not a model.
  3. Estimate = this season's sales + the average of those historical "still to sell" amounts. The error band comes from how far this method missed in each past season.
  4. Regions and milestones = the requirement shared out in proportion to what each region normally buys from this point, and spread over the weeks using the usual seasonal pattern.
  5. Unknown destinations are not assigned to any country: by the end of the season they show up under named buyers, and the history already reflects that.

Data: USDA FAS weekly export sales through week 10 (w/e 5 Nov), 10 seasons. USDA November 2026 WASDE. Full workbook attached.
